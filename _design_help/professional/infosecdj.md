**[DJ Semiconductor Services](http://infosecdj.net/)** is the EU-based
imaging lab of Dmitry "InfoSecDJ" Janushkevich, offering semiconductor
decapsulation, deprocessing, and imaging. If you want to see what actually
came back from the fab — for debugging, documentation, teaching, or simply a
beautiful picture of your chip — Dmitry can photograph it.

### See it on wafer.space silicon

Dmitry photographed the fabricated dies from GF180MCU Run 1 (the picture here
is his micrograph of the wafer.space logo die). Every image is a stitched
panorama of the whole die, published on the run pages:

- **[All Run 1 die photographs](https://wafer.space/ws-run1/#photos)** — 21 designs
- [KIAN](https://wafer.space/ws-run1/projects/KIAN.html#photo) — KianV, a 32-bit RISC-V Linux SoC
- [MOLE](https://wafer.space/ws-run1/projects/MOLE.html#photo) — FABulous FPGA
- [RBOY](https://wafer.space/ws-run1/projects/RBOY.html#photo) — RISCBoy-180
- [GD03](https://wafer.space/ws-run1/projects/GD03.html#photo) — Racquet, a 23 core SoC
- [TQVA](https://wafer.space/ws-run1/projects/TQVA.html#photo) — TinyQV, a crowdsourced RISC-V SoC
- [MOSB](https://wafer.space/ws-run1/projects/MOSB.html#photo) — MOSbiusV3
- [WSLG](https://wafer.space/ws-run1/projects/WSLG.html#photo) — the wafer.space logo die

The [full-resolution files](https://data.wafer.space/infosecdj.net/release-1/)
are free to download, at 200 to 500 MB per die.

### Services

- **Die imaging**: optical microscopy panoramas of effectively unlimited size,
  at up to 1000x magnification, of a whole die or a region of interest
- **Focus stacking**: features at every depth in focus, or multi-focus
  panoramas with each layer in focus separately
- **Decapsulation**: getting the die out of ceramic and epoxy (DIP, QFP, DFN,
  simple BGA) packages
- **Deprocessing**: removing the metal layers that hide the features underneath
- **Delivery**: a single image file, or a web-based deep-zoom dataset for easy
  inspection

Decapsulation and deprocessing can destroy the sample, so plan on sending
several. The optical setup is at its best on process nodes down to about
350nm; the Run 1 photographs show what to expect on GF180MCU.
